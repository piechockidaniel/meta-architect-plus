# Delivery plan

> Scope revision: [Release baseline](RELEASE-BASELINE.md) supersedes earlier provider-selection, hybrid-coverage, hardware and capacity proposals in this document.

Status: proposed sequence and acceptance gates; no milestone is complete merely because its document exists.
Updated: 2026-10-11

## Scope baseline

Deliver a business web application assessment with organizational and greenfield entry paths, a bounded validated catalogue, deterministic constraints and costs, spatial consequence exploration, and matching diagrams and Markdown.

Provider bindings, hybrid support, target hardware, export behavior and AI inclusion remain open. Planning documents establish proposed behavior; they do not establish runtime capability.

## M0 — Resolve feasibility and scope

Work: inspect the live workspace, choose supported device and first catalogue variants, verify current SDK setup, and prototype hands-only start/select/export retrieval. Confirm scope exclusions and evidence policies. Resolve competition questions through the user where organizer clarification is needed.

Exit: supported workload and variants are explicit; device access is known; export retrieval has a demonstrated path; implementation structure is grounded in the workspace. If retrieval fails, resolve it before building a full export UI.

## M1 — Build the decision foundation

Work: implement versioned inputs, catalogue schema, architecture graph, validation findings, cost calculation and immutable snapshots. Add authored sample inputs for both entry paths.

Exit: mandatory constraints cannot be overridden by preferences; unknown and unsupported states remain distinct; meaningful fixtures pass; no incomplete estimate is presented as a complete total.

## M2 — Complete a functional vertical slice

Work: connect intake, comparison, one scenario change, review and export. A simple presentation can establish behavior before spatial polish.

Exit: both samples produce traceable results and consistent artifacts; changes reevaluate correctly; undo and failure recovery preserve confirmed state. The supported core works without external AI.

## M3 — Deliver the immersive decision workflow

Work: implement seated hands-first navigation, aligned alternatives, requirement/component selection, consequence highlighting and accessible state labels.

Exit: on the target device, a user completes either sample without a controller; essential information remains readable and reachable; export retrieval succeeds. First-five-minutes timing is measured, not assumed.

## M4 — Validate usefulness and reliability

Work: test comprehension with prospective users, compare spatial and flat consequence exploration, verify sample costs and evidence, test interruption/resume and sustained device performance. Add AI assistance only if it improves the supported workflow without weakening correctness.

Exit: recorded results identify comprehension, comfort and technical limitations; material issues are fixed or explicitly scoped out. Documentation distinguishes validated behavior from hypotheses.

## M5 — Prepare a reviewable submission

Work: produce an accurate short demonstration, screenshots, submission description, launch/testing instructions and a frozen release candidate. Recheck official rules, eligibility, access requirements and deadline before release.

Exit: the build is accessible, the demonstration represents actual behavior, the submission materials agree with the implemented scope, and the user has reviewed concrete publication/submission artifacts. Public upload and submission are separate user-authorized actions.

## Scheduling approach

Plan backwards from the competition deadline after confirming team capacity and hardware access. Preserve time for real-device testing, fixes and submission preparation. Avoid committing calendar dates for implementation without knowing available working time.

Prioritize completion of both entry paths through one shared vertical slice. Cut breadth before cutting correctness or hands-only completion.

## Scope cuts if delivery is at risk

1. Defer additional providers and hybrid variants.
2. Defer AI extraction and generative explanations; retain authored questions and traceable explanations.
3. Defer elaborate failure animations and additional growth variants.
4. Defer accounts, collaboration and cross-device synchronization.

Retain constraint validation, uncertainty labels, operating responsibilities, consistent exports, both sample entry paths and real-device verification.

## Release evidence checklist

- Actual supported catalogue and evidence snapshot.
- Domain and calculation check results.
- Diagram/document consistency checks.
- Hands-only completion and artifact retrieval on target hardware.
- Readability, comfort and performance observations.
- User comprehension findings and known limitations.
- Build identifier and accurate demonstration assets.
- Current submission requirements and access verification.

## Planning decisions required before implementation

Confirm catalogue coverage and first provider bindings, available hardware, preferred AI boundary, and expected development capacity. Gather this information during implementation preparation rather than silently choosing unsupported capabilities.
